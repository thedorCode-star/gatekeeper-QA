# Gatekeeper QA — Brand Identity

**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Draft  
**Date:** 2026-08-24

---

## Purpose

This document defines how Gatekeeper QA looks, sounds, and feels so the website, proposals, and outreach stay consistent.

---

## Brand Promise

Gatekeeper QA helps SaaS startups release with confidence by finding risk early, making quality visible, and fitting testing into the way the team already ships.

---

## Personality

Professional and calm.

- Trustworthy, not flashy
- Clear, not clever
- Precise, not academic
- Supportive, not salesy

**We are:** a serious quality partner.  
**We are not:** a cheap bug-hunting mill, a vague “AI QA” brand, or a loud startup agency.

---

## Voice and Tone

| Situation | Tone |
|-----------|------|
| Homepage and About | Confident, plain language |
| Services and Process | Specific, outcome-led |
| Technical pages | Competent, jargon only when useful |
| Contact and outreach | Direct, respectful of time |

**Write like this:** “We help you catch release-blocking bugs before customers do.”  
**Not like this:** “We supercharge your QA flywheel with world-class excellence.”

### Voice rules

- Prefer short sentences and concrete verbs
- Name the problem, then the outcome
- Speak to founders first, then CTOs
- Never overclaim (“zero bugs”, “guaranteed quality”)
- Use “we” for the company, “you” for the client

---

## Positioning Line

**Primary:** Gatekeeper QA helps SaaS startups build confidence in their software through professional testing, automation, and quality engineering support.

**Short:** Quality engineering for SaaS teams that need to ship with confidence.

**Even shorter:** Release with confidence.

---

## Messaging Pillars

1. **Trust** — We report honestly, including what we did not test.
2. **Quality** — We focus on risk that can hurt users and revenue.
3. **Fit** — We work with your tools, cadence, and team size.
4. **Clarity** — Founders and engineers both understand the status of quality.

## Visual Direction

### Color

Deep blue plus neutrals. Blue signals reliability; neutrals keep the site calm and easy to read.

| Token | Hex | Use |
|-------|-----|-----|
| Primary | `#1B365D` | Headings, nav, primary buttons |
| Primary hover | `#152A4A` | Button hover |
| Accent | `#2E5A88` | Links, highlights, icons |
| Background | `#F7F8FA` | Page background |
| Surface | `#FFFFFF` | Cards, sections |
| Text | `#1A1F2E` | Body copy |
| Muted text | `#5C6578` | Captions, secondary copy |
| Border | `#D9DEE7` | Dividers, inputs |
| Success | `#2F6F4E` | Positive states only, sparingly |

Do not use neon colors, heavy gradients, or high-saturation red as a brand color.

### Typography

- **Headings:** Inter or a similar geometric sans (bold / semibold)
- **Body:** Inter or system sans (regular)
- Keep line length readable; avoid dense walls of text
- Generous spacing; the brand should feel unhurried

### Imagery and UI

- Clean layouts, lots of whitespace
- Simple diagrams for process, not stock photos of “people at laptops” as the hero
- Screenshots, checklists, and evidence when we have them
- Icons should be simple line icons, not illustrated mascots

### Logo (until a designed mark exists)

- Wordmark: **Gatekeeper QA**
- Do not abbreviate to “GKQA” in customer-facing copy
- Lockup can sit on white or deep blue; text must stay high-contrast

## Audience-Specific Messages

**Founder:** You will know whether quality is a launch risk, what to fix first, and how QA support pays for itself.

**CTO / Engineering lead:** You will get a testing approach that matches your stack, CI, and release cadence — without slowing the team down.

---

## Do and Don’t

**Do**

- Use evidence (process, coverage, findings, case studies)
- Explain trade-offs
- Invite a discovery call as the next step

**Don’t**

- Promise perfection
- Shame teams for missing tests
- Hide what is out of scope
- Use hype, emojis, or slang in brand materials

---

## Website Implications

- Primary CTA: **Book a QA Discovery Call**
- Secondary CTA: **Explore Our Services**
- Hero should answer “what we do” in a few seconds
- Trust comes from process, services, and later case studies — not from slogans
